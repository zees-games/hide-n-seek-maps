# Writing a map

A map is one JSON file in this directory. A server reads every `.json` here at boot (and again on `hs_reload`), checks it, and plays it; a file it refuses is listed by `hs_maps` with the reason, and every other map still plays. Clients never read these files: the server sends the map it is playing to each of them when the map changes.

The maps shipped here are generated by [`../tools/build_maps.py`](../tools/build_maps.py) and should be changed there, not here: `tools/build_maps.py --check` fails when a file is not what it would write. The builder is also the quickest way to write a new map, because a wall with its doors and windows, a room with its floor, ceiling and light, and a furnished classroom are one call each. What follows is the format itself.

Every length is metres, every angle degrees, every time seconds. A position is `[x, y, z]`, y is up, and the floor of a building is at y 0.

## The document

```json
{
	"format": 1,
	"kind": "hideseek_map",
	"id": "hs_my_map",
	"name": "My Map",
	"author": "you",
	"blurb": "One line, shown when the map starts.",
	"round_seconds": 0,
	"hide_seconds": 0,
	"kill_y": -20.0,
	"environment": {
		"sky_top": [0.32, 0.52, 0.82], "sky_horizon": [0.72, 0.82, 0.92], "ground": [0.35, 0.33, 0.30],
		"sun_pitch": -55, "sun_yaw": 30, "sun_energy": 1.1, "sun_colour": [1.0, 0.96, 0.88],
		"ambient": [0.62, 0.66, 0.72], "ambient_energy": 0.55, "fog": 0.0
	},
	"boxes": [],
	"props": [],
	"lights": [],
	"doors": [],
	"toys": [],
	"spawns": {"hiders": [], "seekers": []}
}
```

- `id` is lowercase a to z, 0 to 9 and `_`. A file whose id is `hs_practice` replaces the built-in practice house.
- `round_seconds` and `hide_seconds`: how long this map's rounds and hides last; 0 is the server's own (`hs_round_seconds`, `hs_hide_seconds`), and the server's is always the ceiling.
- `kill_y`: anybody below it has fallen: a hider is caught by it and a seeker starts again. Put it under anything a player could fall off.
- `environment`: every field is optional and the values above are the defaults. `fog` is a density; 0.0025 is a light haze over a big outdoor map, and 0.008 already hides everything past forty metres.

## Boxes

The building: floors, walls, ceilings, roofs, glass, a pool, a painted line.

```json
{"at": [0, 1.5, -8], "size": [12, 3, 0.2], "mat": "paint", "tint": [0.95, 0.93, 0.85], "yaw": 0, "solid": true, "visible": true}
```

- `at` is the box's **centre**; `size` is its width, height and depth before `yaw` turns it.
- `mat` is one of: `wood_floor`, `parquet`, `gym`, `tile`, `tile_check`, `tile_small`, `carpet`, `paint`, `plaster`, `ceiling`, `brick`, `stone`, `concrete`, `sidewalk`, `asphalt`, `grass`, `dirt`, `sand`, `rubber`, `roof`, `wood`, `metal`, `locker`, `chalkboard`, `whiteboard`, `glass`, `water`, `line`, `hedge`, `fabric`, `flat`. An unknown name is drawn as `flat`. Textures are laid in world space, so two boxes of the same material meet without a seam.
- `tint` multiplies the material's colour.
- `"solid": false` for anything a player walks through: water, a painted line, a light's glow.
- `"visible": false` for a collider with nothing drawn: an invisible wall at the edge of the map.

## Props

The furniture, the plants, the rocks: everything a hider gets behind, under or on top of.

```json
{"id": "furniture_chair", "at": [3.6, 0, 1.2], "yaw": 90, "pitch": 0, "roll": 0}
```

- `id` is a prop from game-hide-n-seek's `props/catalogue.json` (`furniture_*` are Kenney's Furniture Kit, `nature_*` the Nature Kit; the file has every id with its size). An id the game does not have is built as nothing and logged.
- `at` is the middle of the prop's **footprint on whatever it stands on**, so a chair is placed by where it stands and a lamp on a table by the table's top.
- A prop's front faces +Z at yaw 0. So against a wall at the smaller z it faces into the room at yaw 0, against one at the larger z at 180, at the smaller x at 90 and at the larger x at -90.
- Props are solid, to players and to a seeker's sight. A crouching person fits behind anything about 0.8 m high and 0.6 m across; `headless_maps` wants a dozen of those on every map.

## Lights

```json
{"at": [0, 2.9, -4], "range": 9, "energy": 1.1, "colour": [1.0, 0.95, 0.85]}
```

An omni light. Indoors, one per room of about eight by eight metres, just under the ceiling. Browsers draw at most eight lights on any one surface, so a big room wants a few strong lights more than many weak ones.

## Doors

Panels a player opens and shuts with the use key (E): a room's door, a cupboard, a gate, a container's pair, a roller shutter, a panel of wall that slides. A shut door is a wall: it stops a body, a seeker's view and a touch. A door is the one thing in a map that moves, and it moves the same on the server and on every client.

```json
{"at": [0, 1.15, -1], "size": [1.37, 2.28, 0.06], "yaw": 0, "motion": "swing", "hinge": "left", "angle": 100,
 "away": true, "open": false, "seconds": 0.6, "travel": 0, "mat": "wood", "tint": [0.55, 0.4, 0.28], "name": "bedroom door", "secret": false}
```

- `at` is the middle of the panel **shut**, and `size` its width, height and thickness, turned by `yaw` like a box. Make it a little smaller than the hole it fills: a centimetre or two round a door, a few millimetres round a secret one.
- `motion` is `swing` (about a hinge at one edge), `slide` (sideways along the wall, into it) or `lift` (straight up, a shutter).
- `hinge` is the edge it swings from, or the way it slides: `left` is the panel's own -x, which with `yaw` 0 is the smaller x and with `yaw` 90 the larger z.
- `angle` is how far a swing opens, in degrees; its sign is the side it opens to. With `away` (the default) the game swings it away from whoever opens it instead, so a door can be opened from either side; set `"away": false` for a door that must only ever go one way (into a room because a sofa is on the other side, out of a stall because the toilet is in).
- `open` is how it stands at the start of every round. `seconds` is how long it takes, and `travel` how far a slide or a lift goes in metres (0 is its whole width or height: a shutter in a tall wall wants less, or it comes out through the roof).
- `name` is what the prompt says: "E  Open the bedroom door".
- `"secret": true` draws no handle. Give it the wall's own `mat` and `tint`, make it nearly the wall's thickness, and slide it: it reads as wall until somebody tries it.
- A door never opens or shuts into anybody: the use is refused while a body stands anywhere it will pass through.

`headless_maps` swings every door both ways it can go and fails one that passes through a wall or a prop, and walks a ray through every doorway with its door open and fails one with something standing in it. In the builder, a door is a hole in a wall with a fifth element: `(3.0, 4.4, 0.0, DOOR_H, {"name": "bedroom door"})`.

## Toys

```json
"toys": [
	{"toy": "beach_ball", "at": [16.5, 0, -2.5]},
	{"toy": "swing", "at": [-17, 0, 6], "yaw": 90},
	{"toy": "lamp_post", "at": [13.5, 0, -5]},
	{"toy": "light_switch", "at": [12, 0, -3], "links": [2]}
]
```

Things players play with, from dot-props' toy catalogue: `beach_ball`, `football`, `bowling_ball`, `bowling_pin`, `barrel`, `tyre`, `punching_bag`, `swing`, `seesaw`, `merry_go_round`, `wobbly_platform`, `trampoline`, `bounce_pad`, `light_switch`, `lamp_post`, `fountain`, `horn`, `bell`. The server simulates them and every client sees them where the server has them. At most 48 on a map.

- `at` is the middle of the toy's foot on the floor; `yaw` turns it (a swing's bar runs along its x; a bounce pad throws along its +z, where its arrow points).
- `links` (a switch only): the indexes in this list of the toys it turns on and off. `on` starts a lamp or a fountain off or on.
- A ball, a pin, a barrel or a tyre is pushed by whoever walks into it, and is no wall: nobody can be stuck on one. Everything else is solid, and the stand-ins' walkable mesh goes round it.
- Every toy goes home at the start of a round.
- A toy the server's dot-props does not have is left out (and `hs_maps` says so); the map still plays.
- Keep a trampoline seven metres clear and a bounce pad's landing seven metres clear along its arrow: a trampoline throws a person 2.3 m up, so beside a low roof it would put them somewhere the map meant to be reached another way, or not at all.

In the builder, `m.toy("swing", x, z, yaw)` places one and moves it to the nearest clear, floored spot within `near` metres (it stops the build when there is none); `bowling(m, x, z, yaw)` sets up ten pins and a ball as one set; `lamps_on_a_switch(m, lamps, switch)` lights a path. Each map's toys are its own `toys_<map>` function, near the end of the builder. `tools/build_maps.py --spots hs_park` prints a plan of the open floor to choose from.

## Spawns

```json
"spawns": {
	"hiders": [{"at": [-5, 0.05, 0], "yaw": 90}],
	"seekers": [{"at": [7, 0.05, 0], "yaw": -90}]
}
```

`at` is where the player's feet go. A map needs at least one of each; with more players than spots, later ones are placed in rings round the first. Give the hiders six or more spread through the map, and the seekers three or more. Seekers are blindfolded while the hiders hide, so where they start matters less than in most games; somewhere in the middle makes the first minute of a round a scramble. Keep spawns clear of props: game-hide-n-seek's `examples/headless_maps.tscn` fails a spawn with no floor under it or with something in the way, and the builder moves its own.

## Checking a map

From game-hide-n-seek:

```bash
./game.sh test headless_maps                                   # every map: wire size, props, spawns, nothing floating, doors, toys clear and still
tools/shot.sh --view=room --doors=open --hs-map-ids=hs_my_map --at=0,1.7,0 --look=5,1,5   # with every door open
tools/shot.sh --view=overview --hs-map-ids=hs_my_map           # look at it from above
tools/shot.sh --view=room --hs-map-ids=hs_my_map --at=0,1.7,0 --look=5,1,5
```

`headless_maps` lists every map it checks by id; add a new one to its `MAPS` list. If the map has stairs or a crawl space, add a climb for it to `headless_climb`'s `CLIMBS`: the stairs are where a document that looks right is wrong by a few centimetres.

## Stairs and crawl spaces

There is no stair or ramp in the format: a flight is boxes. Each step is a slab about 0.22 m higher than the last (a player steps up 0.4 m) and about 0.3 m deep rather than a block down to the floor, so there is room under a flight for the flight below it in a stairwell, or for somebody hiding under the stairs. A crawl space is a gap 1.1 m high: a person crouched is 0.95 m and fits, standing is 1.8 m and does not. The builder has `flight()` and `crawl()` for both.
